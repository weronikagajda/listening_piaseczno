WHAT YOU HEAR

The four halls planned at Energetyczna do not exist yet. Where you stand, the noise model of the website gives the level their cooling would reach at night: the sum over the four halls of 75.5 − 20·log10(distance to the hall + 25), fitted to the 25 values of the permit's noise report to about 1.3 dB. The sound is played at that level. Closer, louder; behind the 40 dB line, below the night limit for a house.

The second site, Okulickiego, has no published layout, so it is drawn but makes no sound here. Generators, low frequency and the screens are not in the model, as they are not in the report. The model is a fit to the report's 25 point values; along the 40 dB line the report draws, it gives 33 to 43 dB. So the number and the drawn line can disagree by a few decibels, and the app tells you where you stand against the drawn line, not against the number.

RECORDINGS

A recording of a working data centre replaces the generated hum. The recording gives the character of the sound; the model still gives its level where you stand. Each file is matched to the hum's loudness, so both follow the same scale and the same 40 dB reference. The level measured where it was made is kept with it, to compare with the model later.

LISTENING LEVEL

Play the 40 dB reference, then set the phone's volume so it sounds as loud as a quiet street at night. Keep the volume there for the walk.

MEASURING THE STREET

Uncalibrated until you compare it once with a sound level meter or the sensor: adjust the offset until both show the same value. A-weighting is approximate. Treat the numbers as indicative.

WALK LOG

While Start is on, each position is kept on this phone only, with the modelled level and, if measuring, the street level. Export it for QGIS. It shows where you walked, so keep it private.

PRIVACY

Your position is used on this phone and sent nowhere. The page keeps nothing after it is closed. The author's home is not on this map.
